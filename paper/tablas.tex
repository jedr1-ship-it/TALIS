\textit{The comparison in words.} Every adolescent of the 2024 ELPI wave lived
through the 27 February 2010 earthquake as a child; what varies is how hard
their municipality was shaken and how old they were when it
struck, grouped as under age one (0--11 months), age one (12--23), age two
(24--35) and ages three to four (36--59). The estimates answer one question. Take
the gap in an outcome at ages 15--18 between two exposure ages, say the
children struck at 24--35 months versus those struck at 0--11 months, among
the municipalities the earthquake hit; take the same gap among the
municipalities it did not hit; the coefficient is the first gap minus the
second. We therefore never compare affected against unaffected municipalities
directly, and never compare cohorts nationally: municipality fixed effects
keep every comparison within a municipality, and cohort fixed effects strip
out everything shared by a birth cohort, including its age at the survey.
All tables estimate versions of
\begin{equation}
Y_{imc} \;=\; \alpha \;+\; \sum_{a}\beta_a\,\bigl(\text{Bin}_a^{\,i}\times D_m\bigr)
\;+\; \gamma_a\,\text{Bin}_a^{\,i} \;+\; \psi_m \;+\; \theta_c \;+\; X_i'\delta \;+\; \varepsilon_{imc}
\end{equation}
where $i$ is an adolescent, $m$ the municipality where the household lived
before the earthquake, and $c$ the birth cohort. $\text{Bin}_a$ marks the
child's age, in months, when the earthquake struck. $D_m$ is a dummy equal to
one when the municipality belongs to the affected zone, the six regions where
the shaking was destructive (V to IX and the Metropolitan Region); in the
dose tables it is replaced by the peak ground acceleration, that is, how
strongly the ground actually shook in the municipality, expressed as a share
of the acceleration of gravity and standardized. $\psi_m$ and $\theta_c$ are
municipality and cohort fixed effects and $X_i$ are household controls.
Standard errors are clustered by municipality and estimates use the 2024
sampling weights.\end{minipage}

\begin{center}
{\scshape The Main Coefficient in Words}
\end{center}

Children who lived through the earthquake at age two (24--35 months old)
reach adolescence (ages 15--18) with better mental health and less anxiety
than children who lived through it before age one (0--11 months) or at ages
three to four (36--59 months). In particular, mental health, measured with
the PHQ-4, an internationally validated index that groups four questions on
low mood, loss of interest, nervousness and uncontrollable worry, is 0.39
points better on the index's 0-to-12 scale, or 0.147 standard deviations
(column 3 of Table~\ref{tab:main}). This is a moderate effect: the index
averages 4.31 points in the sample, so the gap equals 9 percent of the mean
and a third of the 1.11-point divide between girls and boys in the same
sample, while the gap by mother's education is essentially zero. Anxiety, the anxiety half of the same index, shows a
probability of screening positive 7.3 percentage points lower, on a base of
27.3 percent.

The age profile is a U (Figure~\ref{fig:ushape}). The scar concentrates on
children struck before age one, when attachment and stress regulation are
forming, and at ages three to four, when children form their first lasting
memories; that the trough sits at age two is consistent with the window of
infantile amnesia, within which events leave no lasting verbal memory (Terr 1988; Bauer 2007; Alberini
and Travaglia 2017). The heterogeneity is concentrated in girls: among
them the index gap doubles to 0.300 standard deviations
and shows up in both halves of the index, anxiety (13.7 percentage points)
and depression (11.7 points), while among boys every estimate is flat
(Table~\ref{tab:het}). The result is flat in a placebo on outcomes fixed at
birth, stable across every specification and dose, and holds under
permutation inference ($p=0.045$); adjusting for the family of four
outcomes leaves Romano--Wolf $p$-values at 0.10--0.12, so the full-sample
evidence is solid rather than overwhelming and the girls' result is the
sharpest finding.

All tables estimate versions of
\begin{equation}
Y_{imc} \;=\; \alpha \;+\; \sum_{a}\beta_a\,\bigl(\text{Bin}_a^{\,i}\times D_m\bigr)
\;+\; \gamma_a\,\text{Bin}_a^{\,i} \;+\; \psi_m \;+\; \theta_c \;+\; X_i'\delta \;+\; \varepsilon_{imc}
\end{equation}
where $i$ is an adolescent of the 2024 ELPI wave, $m$ the commune of selection of
the household before the earthquake, and $c$ the birth cohort. $\text{Bin}_a$
gives the age at which the 27 February 2010 earthquake struck the child
($a\in\{12$--$23,\,24$--$35,\,36$--$59$ months$\}$; omitted: 0--11 months) and
$D_m$ the seismic dose of the commune: the official affected-zone indicator or,
where stated, the peak ground acceleration of the USGS ShakeMap at the commune
centroid. $\psi_m$ and $\theta_c$ are commune and cohort fixed effects; $\psi_m$
absorbs $D_m$, and $\theta_c$ absorbs everything common to a cohort, including
age at the survey. Each $\beta_a$ is therefore a difference in differences
between exposure-age cohorts, within commune.
\end{minipage}

\begin{center}
{\scshape The Main Coefficient in Words}
\end{center}

The headline estimate of the paper is the age-two interaction in column 3 of
Table~\ref{tab:main}, Panel A: $-$0.147 (standard error 0.069). In words:
comparing communes inside and outside the affected zone, an adolescent whom the
earthquake struck at age two reports 0.147 standard deviations \emph{fewer}
depression--anxiety symptoms at ages 14--18 than one it struck before the first
birthday. The scale behind the standard deviations is concrete: the PHQ-4 runs
from 0 to 12, with a sample mean of 4.31 and a standard deviation of 2.67, so
the coefficient is $-$0.39 points on the raw scale (the unstandardized row of
Table~\ref{tab:robust}), about 9 percent of the mean. Read from the other side
of the U (Figure~\ref{fig:ushape}), children the earthquake caught at ages
three to four --- old enough to form memories of it --- carry 0.103 standard
deviations more symptoms than the age-two trough, and infants carry a similar
excess.

Is that magnitude relevant? Three benchmarks say yes, with one honest caveat.
First, in the anxiety-screen metric the same comparison moves the probability
of a positive GAD-2 screen by 7.3 percentage points on a base of 27.3 percent
--- a 27 percent relative reduction, equal to about six tenths of the full
girl--boy gap in positive anxiety screens in this sample (33.3 versus 21.3
percent). Second, it is of the same order as the cognitive scar this
literature measures: $-$0.17 standard deviations of vocabulary at ages 7--11
in Gillmore (2026) and $-$0.24 at ages 14--18 in Table~\ref{tab:longrun}.
Third, in the continuous-dose metric one sample standard deviation of ground
acceleration (about 14 percent of $g$) moves the PHQ-4 by $-$0.068 standard
deviations (Table~\ref{tab:dose}), so the average affected commune, at 1.7
standard deviations of dose, implies roughly $-$0.11. Among girls the trough
doubles, to $-$0.300 standard deviations (Table~\ref{tab:het}). The caveat:
the gradient survives permutation inference (p $=$ 0.045,
Table~\ref{tab:robust}) and every specification change, but adjusting for the
family of four outcomes leaves Romano--Wolf $p$-values at 0.10--0.12
(Table~\ref{tab:rw}); the full-sample evidence is solid rather than
overwhelming, and the girls' result is the sharpest single finding.
